# Design System: HISEAS Research Atlas

## Direction

HISEAS uses a Field Atlas + Climate Laboratory direction. The interface should feel like a public scientific atlas assembled from real evidence: field imagery, maps, model traces, manuscript figures, and clean explanatory structure.

## Palette

Use OKLCH tokens in CSS. The palette is a light mineral paper field with deep blue-green ink, sea-glass and lichen support colors, and a small coral/rust signal accent. Avoid saturated tropical colors, dark dashboard defaults, beige editorial sameness, gradient text, and glassmorphism.

## Typography

Use a sturdy serif for major place and research hierarchy, currently Georgia as a system-safe placeholder. Use a practical sans for body, labels, and navigation. Labels are uppercase and untracked or minimally tracked; they must carry wayfinding information, not decoration.

## Layout

Prefer asymmetric atlas sections, full-width bands, map/figure panels, and evidence rows. Use cards only for repeated records such as routes, figures, people, and field cases. Avoid nested cards and identical icon-card grids.

## Imagery

Images must reveal actual evidence: reef terraces, field contexts, maps, figures, samples, or model outputs. Captions should name what the viewer is seeing and whether it is published, draft, or field-derived.

## Claim Status

Every emerging result or draft figure needs an explicit status label. Use cautious language for manuscript material: "manuscript in preparation", "draft figure", "working result", and "requires confirmation" where appropriate.
