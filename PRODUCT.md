# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

HISEAS serves several audiences at once: scientists who need to understand the project logic, funders and institutional visitors who need to see coherence and impact, students and public audiences who need a readable path into Last Interglacial sea-level science, and collaborators who want a hub for field campaigns, papers, outreach, and project updates.

## Product Purpose

The site is the public research atlas for HISEAS, a collaborative NSF FRES project asking how much and why ice sheets melted during the Last Interglacial. It should explain the proposal-level scientific arc, surface active field and modeling work, connect publications and findings, and make future stories, photo essays, notes, and outreach links easy to add.

Success means a visitor can understand the central question, the three proposal questions, the evidence-to-model pipeline, the role of field sites such as the Cook Islands, the status of emerging findings, and how HISEAS connects technical sea-level science to public impacts.

## Brand Personality

Field atlas meets climate laboratory.

The site should feel precise, spatial, evidence-rich, and alive with active research. It should use real project artifacts: field photos, reef-terrace observations, maps, GCP/sample notation, manuscript figures, model names, and cautious scientific language.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic university lab pages, empty grant-project boilerplate, climate-dashboard cliches, SaaS-style metric cards, tropical tourism, and overconfident unpublished-result presentation. Cook Islands content should read as field evidence and community-aware research, not destination marketing.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with the research question, then make the machinery visible.
2. Use field evidence, figures, and maps as design material, not decoration.
3. Keep claim status explicit: published, manuscript in preparation, draft figure, field observation, or future work.
4. Let different audiences enter through different doors: overview, methods, field sites, findings, publications, outreach.
5. Preserve uncertainty and provenance in field-derived observations.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA. Use semantic headings, clear focus states, descriptive alt text, readable body text, and non-color-only status labels. Broader impacts and field pages should reflect public engagement, local knowledge exchange, and decolonizing-science commitments without turning them into marketing copy.
